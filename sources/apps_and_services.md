## The organising fact
Everything depends on whether Aaron's data enters China from **outside** the country or **inside** it.
- **Travel eSIM** (Airalo, Nomad, Saily, Holafly) routes through a foreign carrier -> WhatsApp, Gmail, Google Maps, Instagram all work with no VPN. **Profile must be installed before landing** — it can't be downloaded inside the firewall.
- **US carrier roaming** — same offshore routing, day-pass pricing. Good first-hours backup.
- **Local Chinese SIM** — passport, 15–30 min, best rural coverage and cheapest data, but fully firewalled. Only worth it for the Tibet leg, and only if the guide arranges it. **Do not buy one at Xi'an airport on arrival** — that is 2017 advice (Sandra's) and it would kill WhatsApp, which is James's only real channel.
- **Hotel and train Wi-Fi is inside the firewall regardless of SIM.** Connecting to the Westin's Wi-Fi kills WhatsApp until he switches back to cellular.
- **VPN**: buy and test before departure (provider sites blocked inside). 2026 reporting on mainland VPN reliability is mixed after this spring's crackdown on domestic relay servers — treat as spare tyre, not plan.

## Payments
- **Alipay** primary. Visa/Mastercard bind directly. **Free <=¥200, ~3% above** — so small spend on the wallet, big hotel/ticket charges on the card itself. Carries the metro ride code for both Xi'an and Shanghai. Institutional cap $5,000/transaction but issuer channel caps ($1,000–2,000) bind first.
- **WeChat** is not optional — it's a booking channel for several museums and the way people will offer help. Verification is near-instant, unlike Alipay's.
- Prepaid "Tour Pass"-style cards carry ~5% load fees — only if direct binding fails.
- ¥300–500 cash in small bills.

## Maps and transport
- **Amap** (English mode) for transit and walking; Chinese map data is legally offset from GPS, which is why Google Maps misbehaves even when it loads. **Apple Maps works natively** in China on a US iPhone — good fallback.
- **Organic Maps** offline packs for Shaanxi, Shanghai and Tibet (the maintained successor to maps.me, which Sandra's 2017 notes name). On the Tibet leg this is the only mapping to count on.
- **Metro ride codes live inside Alipay** — no separate app in either city, including Line 11 to Disneyland.
- **DiDi** — English, pays via Alipay. Mainly for the PVG run on 15 Oct with the big suitcase.
- **12306** — Z165 already booked via James. Chinese rail is paperless: **board by laying the passport on the gate scanner**, no ticket-window collection. Trip.com is the foreign-card fallback.

## Museum and attraction booking (verified 2026-09-10)
Timed real-name reservation is now the norm and walk-up is largely gone. Channels differ by venue, so check each:
- **Terracotta Warriors** — 7 days ahead *including the booking day*, at **bmy.com.cn** or the museum's WeChat account, both passport-friendly. The museum stated in June 2026 it has **never authorised any third-party platform**, so no OTAs. On-site ticket windows have been removed; passport holders collect at the general service counter. Holiday blocks are released all at once about a week out instead.
- **Shaanxi History Museum** — free but ticketed, 5 days ahead at 17:00 Beijing time. Bookable on the **official website (sxhm.com)** as well as the WeChat mini-programme and WeChat account; the "WeChat account only" line in older notes is out of date. From 15 Mar – 14 Nov 2026 it runs 08:30–19:00, last entry 18:00, **Monday closure suspended**.
- **Shanghai Urban Planning Exhibition Center** — free, timed booking via WeChat mini-programme up to 7 days ahead, closed **Wednesdays**.

## Tibet leg (3–10 Oct)
Coverage falls away outside the Lhasa valley; assume nothing on the Yamdrok/Shigatse road days or at EBC.
- WhatsApp (the operator's real channel) needs offshore data every day of this leg.
- Offline copies before leaving Xi'an: Tibet permit, passport, insurance, hotel confirmations, and the Google Drive "Important Info In China" sheet (Drive is blocked without offshore data).
- Nothing internet-dependent should be scheduled 2–10 Oct. **Exception worth planning around:** the Shanghai Urban Planning Exhibition Center's booking for Sunday 11 Oct opens 4 Oct, mid-tour.
- **Power banks must be bought in China.** Since 28 Jun 2025 the CAAC requires a moulded 3C mark on any power bank taken on a mainland domestic flight; no US-market unit has one, and lithium can't go in the hold — so an uncertified one is confiscated at Lhasa. Buy Xiaomi/Baseus/UGREEN in Xi'an, ~¥100–200.

## Shanghai Disneyland
- Official **Shanghai Disney Resort app** is the only route to Disney Premier Access in-park; registration leans on SMS verification that foreign numbers handle unevenly — finish setting it up on a local network from the Courtyard, not from home.
- **Buy tickets through the official site/app/WeChat/Fliggy first** — Early Bird and bundle pricing exist only there. If the checkout rejects the foreign card or walls you behind SMS, **Klook or Trip.com** is the fallback: Disney's own channels page lists "official travel partners" alongside its direct channels, and they avoid the 3% wallet surcharge over ¥200. Link to the app by passport number or by scanning the ticket QR after entry.

## Language and everything else
- **Google Translate** works only over offshore data (Google withdrew its mainland service) — but download the offline Chinese pack: camera translation on menus and signs works offline.
- **Pleco** — fully offline dictionary, the reliable one at altitude. **Baidu Translate** as a local-network fallback.
- **Dianping** for restaurants; skip **Meituan** (Chinese number + domestic bank card).
- Bonvoy, Delta, Korean Air, China Eastern apps all work; screenshot boarding passes.
- **Online arrival card** (National Immigration Administration site / "移民局12367" app / WeChat or Alipay mini-program) is **optional**, free, and saves time in the arrivals hall. Any site charging for it is a scam.
